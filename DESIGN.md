# Kitt dashboard / consumer design direction

The overview answers four questions in order: how is my car doing, what is this charge costing, what have I used, and where have I driven?

Kitt's own image and API name anchor the page. Charge status keeps the cost prediction and smart schedule together, rather than creating separate forecast panels. Cost and energy totals use clear units and never turn missing history into a £0 result. Vehicle identification and technical connection messages live behind Vehicle and preferences, leaving the home view calm.

Recent journeys use a charcoal map surface, subdued roads, soft water/park shapes and three complementary route colours: lime (`#b6f18b`), ice blue (`#8bcde0`) and lavender (`#b9a0e8`). The journey list and route overlays share colours; selecting or hovering a journey isolates that route. Origins have outlined markers; destinations have solid markers. The layout stacks on mobile. The default view has an honest empty state, and an explicit preview toggle shows fictional routes without inserting them into real totals.

The current map is a **schematic design**, not a georeferenced street map. Its preview places and routes are invented examples. The optional Trip.route field contains SVG view-space points for the presentation layer, not raw latitude/longitude. Before showing real routes, implement location capture, trip segmentation, and a geographic basemap/projection. Existing Prometheus samples contain no GPS, so historical routes cannot be recreated. Location access requires a new Tesla owner consent; no permission change is made by this design update. No map-provider requests or location sharing occur.

Past charging and journey history are still missing data pipelines, rather than design gaps. Prometheus records vehicle measurements today, but there is no completed-session reconstruction or GPS journey recorder. These should be completed before calculating historical cost-per-mile or claiming a month of ownership costs. Initial live cost-per-mile is therefore unavailable. Appearance and LFP chemistry on the Vehicle page use the owner's supplied car description; live name, battery state, odometer, software and cabin readings come from Tesla.

Delivered UI changes: Overview, Charging, Journeys and Vehicle views; compact charge prediction; smart charging timeline; map/list interaction; cost and energy summary; charging history and tariff sections; saved appearance details; connection/estimate explanations in preferences. Existing charging animation and reduced-motion treatment are retained.

Fullscreen desktop pass: at 1360px wide and 820px high or larger, Overview uses the whole viewport with an 88px navigation rail, car and four live metric tiles above a wide journey map, and a full-height charge status column. Historical detail remains in the dedicated views. Clock, last-update time and stale-reading status support unattended display use. Smaller windows retain the scrolling layout. Verified in Chrome at 1366x900, 1920x900, 1920x1080 and 2560x1440 with no document overflow; route previews and the smart schedule remain visible.

The Charging view now imports six months of Octopus home sessions, with UK start/end times, energy, estimated historical SMART costs and a CSV export. A six-month reporting option is the default. The session energy is provider-reported, not reconstructed from Prometheus. Unknown costs remain unavailable and the summary states how many sessions are unpriced. No public charging, losses or standing charges are included in these home estimates.

Appearance now follows the operating system preference by default, with System / Light / Dark choices in the header. A manual choice is stored per browser origin. System mode listens for changes while the dashboard is open. Initial theme is applied before the body renders. The light palette covers the map, charging timeline, history, vehicle, controls and preferences while preserving the dark theme.

Charging focus: while Tesla reports Charging, the main panel and overview tiles show the live session. Otherwise they show the latest completed Octopus home charge by completion time, skipping zero-energy plug-ins and future periods. Last-charge cost, energy, UK start/end times and session window are explicit; current battery is secondary, and upcoming Octopus slots appear only when published. Saved-history and missing-cost states remain labelled. Session window includes pauses and is not active charging duration. History publication may lag.

The appearance control is now one icon-only button, cycling System -> Light -> Dark -> System. Monitor, sun and moon icons identify the current preference; tooltip and accessible name describe the selected mode and next click. Manual choice persists locally. This change and the last-charge focus remain workstation-only until deployment is explicitly requested. Development recording checks use the existing NAS backend, which reads NAS Prometheus; direct read-only PromQL checks can be made through an SSH shell because NAS TCP forwarding is disabled.

Charging is a history-only view, independent of live vehicle-data rendering. It shows recorded cost, energy, session count and an energy-weighted unit cost using priced sessions only. Car imagery, live battery/charging and forward tariff prices are omitted. History has its own loading/stale status and refresh action, so a live-car error does not obscure historical records. The desktop history panel fills the available viewport with a scrollable table; period filtering and CSV export remain available. This change is local only.

The Journeys view now shows cost of the last (or selected) journey, net energy used, distance and duration. An energy timeline sits above the route map; green interval bars represent consumption and blue bars represent recovered energy, using one proportional scale and UK timestamps. Route selection updates the facts and timeline together. No samples are inferred from trip totals. Journey estimates use a supplied cost or the effective unit cost of the most recent recorded charge preceding the journey; the latter is explicitly an estimate, not a precise battery-cost allocation. Missing journey history or interval readings stays empty. A clearly labelled fictional preview demonstrates all visuals without entering the real dataset. Current NAS Prometheus gauges contain no journey energy samples and there is still no drive reconstruction or GPS recorder. The changes are workstation-only.

Vehicle screen (workstation only): focused on saved vehicle identity, odometer, battery/range, software, condition fields and appearance. Charging and journeys have their own screens. Persist per-field vehicle observations in ignored runtime/vehicle-snapshot.json (NAS /config after an explicitly requested deployment). Sleeping/offline/failed polls preserve readings and timestamps; they never publish stale readings as new Prometheus samples. Recover odometer/battery/range from Prometheus for existing installations. Local snapshot seeded read-only from NAS Prometheus, with Kitt identity read from Tesla's vehicle endpoint; no wake requested. No NAS containers modified. Battery health remains unmeasured without capacity and baseline readings; tyre pressures/locks/Sentry populate when provided.
Vehicle range comparison: keep rated remaining range and Tesla's driving-condition estimate side by side inside the existing range card. Real-world estimate uses charge_state.est_battery_range; rated range uses battery_range. Preserve independent observation times and last known values during sleep/offline periods. Never derive real-world range by applying a fixed discount to rated range. Missing estimates show Awaiting Tesla estimate. Add the separate tesladash_real_world_range_miles gauge only when that poll supplies it, with snapshot recovery supported. Development change only; the existing NAS collector must be updated by an explicitly requested deployment before it can supply the new field continuously.
